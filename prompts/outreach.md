# Outreach messages

Agents draft; the user sends. Fill every bracket with a real detail, such as a fact from the person's profile, a recent post, or the company's actual work. Never invent one.

There are two reasons to message someone, and they need different messages.

## 1. There's an open role you're applying to

Use **specific observation → link to your work → small ask**.

- Open with something specific about the team, the product or the role.
- Connect it to one result of yours, with a number.
- Ask for very little: 10–15 minutes, or "open to a quick chat?"
- Keep it under about 300 characters, so it fits a connection request.

For a referral from someone you know, name the job and its ID, attach the tailored resume, and make it easy: "If you're comfortable, I'd love a referral for [role, ID]. I'll send anything you need."

## 2. No open role yet: get on their radar

People like talking about their own path. Get curious about how they got the role and what the day-to-day is like, then ask for a little of their time. The opener depends on who's reading:

- **A peer doing the job you want:** say you noticed what they do at the company, ask how they ended up there, mention that you work in a similar space, and ask about the day-to-day. *"Open to a quick chat sometime?"*
- **A hiring manager:** say you don't see an open role on their team, but you've been following the company's work in a specific area. Give your specialty in a phrase and ask for 10–15 minutes to share your experience.
- **A recruiter:** recruiters focus on the roles they're filling now, so say plainly that you know nothing fits yet but want to stay on their radar. Give your specialty in a phrase, name a problem the company is working on, and ask for 10–15 minutes.

Sending the wrong version to the wrong person reads as generic or transactional, so check who the reader is first.

## Style

- Plain words. No internal jargon from your current job.
- One number at most.
- No "I hope this finds you well" or "great to connect".
- Follow up once after 7 days, then let it go.

The method follows Mikelle's [UNLISTED](https://mikellecareers.com) networking guide.
