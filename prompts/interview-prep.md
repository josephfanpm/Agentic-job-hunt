# Interview prep

## Story bank (build once, reuse everywhere)

From the resume and `private/profile.md`, write 8–10 stories in this shape: **situation → what I did → result (a number) → what I learned**. Cover:

- A product you launched end to end
- A bet backed by data that paid off
- An experiment that failed, and what you did next
- A conflict with engineering, design or a stakeholder
- A time you said no, or cut scope
- Working with customers or users directly
- Something you built with AI
- Leading without authority

Tag each story with the skills it shows, so you can pick the right one quickly.

## For each interview

1. **Read the job description and the company's recent news.** Write 3 sentences: what the team owns, the metric they likely care about most, and the problem you'd attack first.
2. **Match stories to the job's requirements**, one strong story per requirement.
3. **Product sense:** practice one product design question and one metric question in the company's own product area.
4. **Questions to ask:** prepare 3, about the team's biggest open problem, how success is measured in the first 6 months, and how product and engineering decide together.

## How to answer

- **Keep each answer to about 90 seconds**, then stop and let them ask more. Long answers are the most common reason for losing a screen.
- **Start with the result**, then explain how you got there.
- **Location:** say yes to relocating. Don't name a preferred city unless they ask, because a preference can count you out of the other locations.
- **Salary:** let them give a number first. If pushed, give a range that starts at the user's floor.

## After each interview

Within 24 hours, write down the questions, what landed and what didn't, and update the story bank. Set the Applied row's Status, and add a follow-up date.
