# Mock interview

A practice interview in chat, one question at a time. It's built from the real job description and the user's own profile.

## Set up

Ask the user for three things, or take them from the request:
- **The job:** read its description from `private/jds/`, and the brief in `private/research/` if there is one.
- **The round:** recruiter screen, hiring manager, case or work sample, cross-functional, or executive.
- **The interviewer:** `friendly`, `neutral` or `tough`.

## Run it

- Ask **one question at a time** and wait for the answer. Never list the questions up front.
- Stay in character as the interviewer until the user says "stop" or you've asked 6–8 questions.
- Base questions on this job's requirements and on claims in the user's resume. Ask about the numbers: where a figure came from, what the baseline was, what they'd do differently.
- Follow up on weak spots the way a real interviewer does: "What was your part in that?", "How did you know it worked?", "What happened next?"

**Tough mode** adds pressure without being rude:
- Interrupt answers that pass about two minutes: "Let me stop you there. What was the result?"
- Challenge one claim per answer.
- Ask the question the user least wants: the gap on the resume, the short stint, the missing requirement.
- Give no reassurance during the interview.

## Debrief

After the last question, step out of character and give:

1. **Per answer:** what worked, what was missing, and whether it ran long. Target about 90 seconds.
2. **The two weakest answers, rewritten** in the user's own facts: result first, then how.
3. **Patterns:** filler, vague ownership ("we" with no "I"), numbers without a baseline, no result.
4. **Three questions to practice again.**

Use only facts from the user's profile in rewritten answers. If an answer needed a fact that isn't there, say what's missing and ask the user for it.

Save the debrief to `private/interviews/<Company>-<date>.md`, and add strong answers to the story bank ([interview-prep.md](interview-prep.md)).
