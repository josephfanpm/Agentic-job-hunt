# Compare and negotiate offers

## Collect the facts first

For each offer, get these in writing from the offer letter or recruiter. Ask the user for anything missing; never estimate it.

- Base pay, and the currency
- Bonus: target percentage, and what it has actually paid out
- Equity: type (RSUs or options), number of units or dollar value, vesting schedule, cliff, and for options the strike price and latest valuation
- Sign-on bonus, and any clawback terms
- Benefits that have a cash value: retirement match, health premiums, stipends
- Level and title, location, days in office, start date
- For a move to another country: visa type, who pays for it, and relocation support

- Convert everything to one currency, and state the exchange rate and its date.
- Value public-company RSUs at today's price. For private-company equity, show two cases side by side: worth nothing, and worth the last valuation. Don't blend them into one number.
- Note what the numbers leave out: cost of living, tax differences, commute, visa risk. Don't model taxes; tell the user to check with an accountant for a cross-border move.

Then list the non-money differences: scope, manager, team stability, growth, and what the user said they wanted in `private/profile.md`.

## Negotiate

1. **Know the range.** Look up pay for this company, level and location on public sources, and give the source and date.
2. **Pick the ask.** One number above the user's target, with a reason tied to the role or a competing offer.
3. **Order of asks:** level first (it moves everything else), then base, then equity, then sign-on, which is the easiest for a company to give.
4. **Draft the message** for the user to send: thanks, real enthusiasm, the specific ask, the reason, and a clear "if we can get there, I'm ready to sign." Under 120 words.

Rules:
- Never invent a competing offer or inflate current pay. If the user has no other offer, negotiate on market data and the role's scope.
- Negotiate after the written offer, not before.
- The user decides and sends. Present the trade-offs; don't choose for them.
